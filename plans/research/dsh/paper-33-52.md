# Pages 33–52: Transitions in Progress & Metatheory

## §4.3 Transitions in Progress (p.33)
The calculus is scheduler-free: steps are any sequence of rule applications, so every theorem holds for any runtime scheduling policy. Four extensions drop the idealization that transitions are atomic, immediate, infallible; each needs states a transition can occupy mid-flight.

**Def 49 (lifecycle states):** Θ_Γ := Inactive(ζ) | Reloading(i,g,ω) | Active(g,ω) | Unloading(g,ω,ζ), where i is the remaining effect iterator, g the inverse accumulator built so far, ω the committed view (key→provider), ζ the outcome (⊥ or error ξ∈Ξ). A fiber is *installed* when not Inactive; *failed* when Inactive(ξ). Quiescence (eq. 45) re-read over the wider space. Crucially σ_γ (the coeffect table union) still covers Active fibers only — a transitioning fiber reads coeffects through its held ω and provides nothing; a key its own transition already wrote is not yet activatable-against. Figure 2 draws the lifecycle; two transition states (Reloading, Unloading) are outlined.

## §4.3.1 Withdrawal (p.34–35)
Deactivation must span an interval: a teardown (e.g., closing a connection pool) may still need the coeffect being withdrawn. **Def 50:** relied_n(γ) — some other installed fiber's committed view resolves a key to n. **L-Leave** moves Active→Unloading: records the decision, stops providing coeffects, keeps its own committed view. **L-Unload** (guarded by ¬relied_n and target g(γ)=δ) applies the accumulator, discards the view, lands Inactive(ζ) — the *only* rule that runs an accumulator. Visibility is carried by the committed view; ordering by the ¬relied guard, which holds withdrawal until every consumer resolving to n is gone. Guard is per-binding, not per-fiber. Deadlock is avoided because Unloading fibers leave σ_γ, so no target view can newly name them (Thm 66 shows the guard always releases). The guard orders along coeffects, not the fiber tree: a parent may unload while a child is still Unloading.

## §4.3.2 Iteration (p.35–36)
**Def 51 (effect iterator E^iter):** recursive type; each iteration yields (δ new context, g inverse, o continuation: Nothing=done, Just(i)=next); the witnessed variant carries proof g(δ)≃γ. **Def 52 (effect^iter_Γ):** composes each inverse onto the accumulator in application order, so φ∘g₁∘…∘g_k undoes in LIFO order; lands in ∂Γ→∂²Γ, so an iterator *is* an effect and usable wherever one is. The Maybe continuation gives a boundary between iterations — a reified delimited continuation mapping directly onto languages' `yield`/generators; **the implementation admits an iterator at every mutation site (§5.1.1)**. Rules: **L-Begin** (Inactive(⊥), target≠⊥ → Reloading(e_n, id_Γ, ω)); **L-Iter** (target unchanged, iterate, compose g∘h); **L-Finish** (Nothing → Active); **L-Divert** (target changed → Unloading(g∘h,ω,⊥)), with two alternatives: abort the held iteration, or let it land first (§4.3.3). Diverts fall only at iterator granularity. A plain effect function is the degenerate one-iteration case: all-or-nothing installation.

## §4.3.3 Asynchrony (p.37)
Non-immediacy modeled abstractly by Future(A): between launch and landing, external state may change. The layer adds *inertia* — a launched iteration must land, cannot be declined — as a restriction on which L-Divert alternative a host may take (no new rules or types). The in-flight iteration lands, then the fiber deactivates via Unloading (routing through Active would wrongly offer coeffects for one step). **This is the mutual chaining of `reload` and `unload` in the implementation.** L-Unload has no target-view premise, so deactivation may chain straight into a fresh L-Begin.

## §4.3.4 Failure (p.37–38)
**E^fail (eq. 49):** iterations may raise Left(ξ)∈Ξ instead of yielding. **L-Raise:** Reloading→Unloading(g,ω,ξ) — recovers before recording; accumulator up to the failing iteration applies at L-Unload; fiber lands Inactive(ξ) having installed nothing. Every outcome is reachable only through Unloading (Thm 59 turns on this). L-Begin requires Inactive(⊥), so a failed fiber is not retried against an unchanged environment; it obstructs nothing (no committed view). **Failure is recorded per-fiber, not propagated to the parent — a failing component leaves siblings running, "the behavior a plugin host wants."**

## §4.4 Metatheory (p.38–41)
Ten rules total (3 orchestration + 7 lifecycle). **Def 53:** index steps t; episode of n = maximal interval where installed holds. Every step factors as γ^{t+1}=edit^t(Ψ^t(γ^t)) (eq. 52): **state map Ψ^t** (pr₁∘i at L-Iter/L-Finish/landing L-Divert; g at L-Unload; id_Γ otherwise) vs. **edit** on control fields. Tables σ_m vs. control fields (θ,τ,π,d,p,e) partition writes; ≈ = agreement on everything but control fields; ≃ (eq. 53) refined to include control fields. **Table 1** tabulates all ten rules as writes. **Lemma 54:** locality — tables move only inside Ψ at the acting fiber; ω exists exactly during an episode; only L-Unload applies an accumulator; installedness flips only at L-Begin/L-Unload; π,d,p,e write-once, τ monotone. **Lemma 55 (≃-invariance):** rules can't see below ≃ — the calculus descends to Γ/≃. **Lemma 56 (equivariance):** bijective renaming of names commutes with steps. **Lemma 57 (vestigial entries):** a stripped entry is invisible to all rules — enables retiring fibers and Lemma 72's deletions. Dropping §4.3.1's guard breaks Def 58(3,4) and Thm 63; the other three subsections simplify away safely.

## §4.4.1 Preservation (p.42–43)
**Def 58 (well-formed registry):** parent pointers land in registry; provisions pairwise disjoint (single provider per key); installed ⇒ ω total on d, valued in the registry; resolved providers are installed. **Thm 59 (Preservation):** well-formedness is preserved by every rule. The L-Unload guard carries clauses 3–4. Consequences: freed names may be reissued; a fiber may be removed as soon as Inactive with no dependency check.

## §4.4.2 Temporal Composability (p.43–45)
**Def 60:** reach(i), transformation monoid M(i) over an iterator's forward maps and inverses; *independence* of iterators = commuting maps + iterations yielding ≃-equal triples at commuted states. **Thm 61 (Recovery exactness):** under pairwise independence, applying n's accumulator at γ^u yields (≈) the state the interleaved *other-fiber* steps alone would have produced — running an inverse withdraws exactly that fiber's contribution. **Cor 62 (Terminal recovery):** a closed episode leaves the state as if n never ran, whatever outcome. Coeffect operations of §3.2 need no independence hypothesis (set-ops on disjoint keys commute).

## §4.4.3 Spatial Composability (p.45–47)
**Thm 63 (Ordering):** activation only where dependencies provided; a consumer's provider stays Active(-ly resolvable) throughout the consumer's episode; provider's unload strictly follows (u′<u). **Thm 64 (Resolution coherence):** every iteration of a transition runs against the one resolution ω fixed at L-Begin; on leaving Reloading either L-Finish→Active(-,ω) or L-Divert/L-Raise with terminal recovery. Inertia makes this a disjunction. The committed view records the *provider*, which is what survives scoped resolution (§3.2.3); **the implementation holds the view in `fiber.committed` (§5.1.3)**.

## §4.4.4 Progress (p.47–48)
**Def 65:** precedence n≺m := p_n∩d_m≠∅ (orders activations, not lifetimes). **Thm 66 (Progress):** with ≺ acyclic, len(e_n)≤K, N finite: (1) *No deadlock* — non-quiescence implies some lifecycle rule applies (the Unloading chain construction shows the relied guard always releases); (2) *Termination* — S(n)≤(K+4)(V(n)+1), all finite; every maximal sequence quiesces. Finiteness of N holds when no component registers instances of itself unboundedly (registration trees of bounded depth, branching ≤K).

## §4.4.5 Confluence (p.49–52)
Headline: **dynamic history leaves no trace** — the quiesced state equals what a static, once-in-dependency-order assembly would produce; the analogue of incremental computation's from-scratch consistency (relevant to HMR/config-reconciliation semantics). **Def 67:** support relation ⊲ (≺ ∪ parent), support set A — a function of inputs (τ,π,d,p), not schedule. **Lemma 68:** ⊲ well-founded, A unique. **Def 69 (total on provision):** dom(σ_n)=p_n when Active. **Lemma 70:** at unfailed quiescence, A = the Active fibers. **Lemma 71 (Transposition):** adjacent independent steps commute. **Lemma 72 (Deletion):** a closed episode plus its registrations can be excised, reaching the same γ^T. **Thm 73 (Confluence):** (1) *Canonical form* — γ^T is reached by orchestration steps first, then one episode per supported fiber in ⊲-linearized order; (2) any two sequences with the same orchestration steps reach states equal up to renaming, ≃, and ≈.
