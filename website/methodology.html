How the score works

AMC scores what your agent did, not what it says

The score asks 244 questions about how an agent is run. Above L1, a question earns a level only from records AMC made itself while it watched the agent work. What the agent or its makers say about it can reach L1 at most.

Current methodology 2026.10.08-r226, released 2026-10-08. amc methodology prints it.

L2, and only 4 of the 244 questions can reach it. No question can reach L3, L4 or L5 yet.

What it is not
Not a certificate and not a legal finding. A signed score shows who wrote it and that it has not changed.

The six levels, and what each needs

Levels build on each other: a question reaches a level only if it passes every level below it. A level AMC cannot check yet is reported as not evaluated, never as passed.

  1. L0 Absent
  2. L1 Initial
  3. L2 Developing
  4. L3 Defined
  5. L4 Managed
  6. L5 Optimizing
  • L0 Absent. Nothing recorded yet. Every question starts here.
  • L1 Initial. At least two records about the question. What the agent or its makers report about themselves reaches this level and no higher.
  • L2 Developing. Enough records AMC observed itself, across sessions and days. Today only 4 of the 244 questions have the records this needs.
  • L3 Defined. Not evaluated yet: AMC does not yet record the runtime evidence this level needs for any question.
  • L4 Managed. Not evaluated yet: it waits for anchored records and a sampling record.
  • L5 Optimizing. Not evaluated yet: it waits for independent, signed attestations, which AMC does not issue yet.

So a score today runs from L0 to L2. Treat L3, L4 and L5 as where the scale is going, not as levels an agent can reach now. The full rules are in the scoring methodology.

Five areas, 244 questions

An area's level is a weighted average of its questions' levels. The overall level is the average of the five areas.

Strategic Agent Operations

19 questions on the agent's purpose, scope and how it is run.

Leadership & Autonomy

23 questions on what it may do alone and who must approve what.

Culture & Alignment

95 questions on safety, honesty and openness about what it does.

Resilience

55 questions on handling errors and attacks, and noticing when its behaviour changes.

Skills

52 questions on memory, reasoning, knowing how sure it is and getting facts right.

A longer set of 264 questions adds questions on running, proof, memory and several agents working together. The same level rules apply to it.

Which records count

Every record carries the source it came from. The source decides how high a question can go; it does not multiply the level.

  • Observed by AMC. AMC saw the action itself, for example through its gateway or its own agent. Counts toward L1 and L2 when one of AMC's own recorders wrote it.
  • Attack tests AMC ran. Tests AMC controls in a sandbox. Counts the same way as observed records.
  • Signed by someone else. An outside party's signed statement. Supports no level yet: nothing AMC records writes these today.
  • Self-reported. The agent's or its makers' own claims, and imported results. L1 at most.

Records AMC observed stop counting toward a level once they are older than 90 days, so a score needs recent work. AMC also uses numeric weights for these sources (1.1, 1.0, 0.8 and 0.4) in two other checks, the formal-specification check and the drift check. They do not set a question's level.

What a score will not tell you

  • That the agent is safe, correct or lawful. A score says what AMC recorded. People judge the agent.
  • That a signed score is true. A signature shows who wrote the report and that it has not changed since.
  • That you meet a framework. AMC lines records up with 22 frameworks, such as the EU AI Act, ISO/IEC 42001 and the NIST AI RMF. It does not certify legal compliance.
  • That industry answers were checked. The 41 Industry Packs are self-reported questionnaires. Their content is experimental and AI-drafted until a named expert signs it.

Beyond the questions

Attack tests

143 registered assurance packs: sets of attack tests, for example whether an agent fakes good behaviour, holds back what it can do, or works against its instructions in secret.

Claims against records

The gap between what an agent's papers claim and what its records support can be large. In one worked example it was 84 points: the 84-point gap. That is one example, not an average.
