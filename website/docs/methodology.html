Scoring methodology

AMC scores an agent from records of what it did, not from what it or its makers say about it. This page is the short version; the approach page and the full scoring methodology have the rest.

Current methodology 2026.10.08-r226, released 2026-10-08. amc methodology prints it with its hash.

Where scores stand today

The highest level AMC gives is L2, and only 4 of the 244 questions can reach it. No question can reach L3, L4 or L5 yet. A new agent starts at L0.

Five areas, 244 questions

The score asks questions about how an agent is run, in five areas. An area's level is a weighted average of its questions' levels; the overall level is the average of the five areas.

AreaQuestionsWhat it asks about
Strategic Agent Operations19The agent's purpose, scope and how it is run.
Leadership & Autonomy23What it may do alone and who must approve what.
Culture & Alignment95Safety, honesty and openness about what it does.
Resilience55Handling errors and attacks, and noticing when its behaviour changes.
Skills52Memory, reasoning, knowing how sure it is and getting facts right.

A longer set of 264 questions adds questions on running, proof, memory and several agents working together. The same level rules apply to it.

Six levels

Levels build on each other: a question reaches a level only if it passes every level below it. A level AMC cannot check yet is reported as not evaluated, never as passed.

  • L0 Absent. Nothing recorded yet. Every question starts here.
  • L1 Initial. At least two records about the question. What the agent or its makers report about themselves reaches this level and no higher.
  • L2 Developing. Enough records AMC observed itself, across sessions and days.
  • L3 Defined, L4 Managed, L5 Optimizing. Not evaluated yet. Treat them as where the scale is going, not as levels an agent can reach now.

Which records count

Every record carries the source it came from. The source decides how high a question can go; it does not multiply the level.

  • Observed by AMC. AMC saw the action itself, for example through its gateway or its own agent. Counts toward L1 and L2 when one of AMC's own recorders wrote it.
  • Attack tests AMC ran. Tests AMC controls in a sandbox. Count the same way as observed records.
  • Signed by someone else. An outside party's signed statement. Supports no level yet: nothing AMC records writes these today.
  • Self-reported. The agent's or its makers' own claims, and imported results. L1 at most.

Records AMC observed stop counting toward a level once they are older than 90 days, so a score needs recent work. AMC also uses numeric weights for these sources (1.1, 1.0, 0.8 and 0.4) in two other checks, the formal-specification check and the drift check. They do not set a question's level.

What a signature shows

AMC signs its records and reports with keys kept in the workspace's vault, and links each record to the one before it, so a change after signing shows up when the record is checked. A signature shows who wrote a record and that it has not changed since. It does not show that the record is true. Someone who can rewrite both the workspace and AMC's own folder on that computer can still roll records back, unless the log was anchored somewhere public.

Whether there are enough records behind a score is reported separately as evidence readiness. Only a score whose readiness reads READY should back a claim outside your team.

What a score will not tell you

  • That the agent is safe, correct or lawful. A score says what AMC recorded. People judge the agent.
  • That you meet a framework. AMC lines records up with frameworks such as the EU AI Act, ISO/IEC 42001 and the NIST AI RMF. It does not certify legal compliance.
  • That industry answers were checked. The 41 Industry Packs are self-reported questionnaires. Their content is experimental and AI-drafted until a named expert signs it.

Check it yourself

amc methodology                # the current method, its version and its hash
amc diagnostic bank verify     # the question set is signed and unchanged
amc diagnostic render --agent default --format md   # the questions, worded for your agent
amc archetype list             # the agent kinds AMC can word questions for

To check the whole workspace in one pass, run amc verify all --expect-monitor <monitor key sha256> with the monitor key fingerprint you recorded outside the workspace. Without that pin the ledger check reports it as unanchored and the command exits 1.
