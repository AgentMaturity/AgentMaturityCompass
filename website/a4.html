A4 Forge · preview

Build one AI agent in four steps, with a person's sign-off on each

A4 Forge is AMC's guided build. You answer plain questions, AMC drafts the agent with you, and the people you name approve each step before it moves on. Every step goes into a signed record you can check later.

For agents someone will ask about later

A4 Forge is for teams in regulated work who must later show how an agent was made: who asked for it, what it should and should never do, who approved each step, exactly which version they approved, and what the operator says was deployed.

It keeps the record of how the agent was built. It does not score or judge the agent: AMC's score is a separate tool, and people judge the agent.

The four steps

Every step works the same way. You answer its questions. AMC says back what it understood, and you confirm it. AMC explains its choices and drafts a plan. People approve the direction, AMC builds, someone reviews the result, and people approve it as done. If you change an answer later, AMC makes a new version, and an approval still open on the old one no longer counts.

1 Aspire

Say what the agent is for

You answer questions about the problem, who the agent serves, what success looks like, what it must never do, and how risky the work is. Start from an Industry Pack and AMC fills in the sector, a risk level and the rules to check, for you to correct.

AMC writes down your answers, its reading of them as you confirmed it, and the approved brief as a numbered version.

Who signs off

To start building
One person
To finish the step
One person

Never the person who wrote the brief or asked for the approval, except in a practice project.

2 Assemble

Build it

You say what the agent may do on its own and what needs a person's yes, which tools it uses, and which AI model builds it.

AMC builds it in one closed session and records what ran. On the built-in stand-in model, which gives canned replies and needs no key, the record says so: the tools were seen working, and the agent's behaviour was not judged.

Who signs off

To start building
One person
To finish the step
Two different people, neither of whom built it

3 Adapt

Fit it to your rules

You say where the agent runs, whose data it touches, which regions and laws apply, and how long data is kept. What you do not know stays unknown: AMC never guesses it.

AMC drafts the controls for those facts and lists which rules are met and which are still open, as a count and never a percentage. Its legal notes are experimental: an AI drafted them and no lawyer has reviewed them.

Who signs off

To start building
Two people
To finish the step
Two people other than the one who asks, each a workspace owner or auditor, and at least one of them an auditor

That approval says the work was seen. It is not an expert review.

4 Activate

Release it

You choose Docker Compose or Helm, the address the agent answers on, and how you will update, watch and roll it back.

AMC prepares a signed release package. Your operator deploys it and signs a note that they did, and AMC checks that the address answers. AMC never deploys by itself.

On a laptop the project ends after Adapt: AMC refuses the release approval unless AMC itself runs under Docker Compose or Helm.

Who signs off

To release
Two people
To finish the step
Two people

Who approves

The people you name in your workspace. A step does not move on until they approve the exact version in front of them.

  • The person who wrote a step, asked for its approval or built it cannot approve it.
  • Each approval must come from a different person.
  • A second person in your own workspace keeps duties apart. It is not an independent review: AMC cannot show that two accounts belong to two real people.
  • A whole project needs you and two more people who can approve, for example one more workspace owner and one person who may approve and audit.

Trying it alone? Start a practice project

Under Who approves each step? choose Just me, as practice. You approve your own steps, and each one is marked self-approved in the record. Alone you can finish Aspire and build in Assemble. Assemble's final approval needs two different people, so that is as far as one person gets.

The project stops being practice as soon as a second person can sign in. Do not use a practice project for real work.

Starting from an Industry Pack? Add a second person

Every pack is rated high risk or above, so nobody approves their own step. As the workspace owner, choose Add a person in the Compass Console: it creates their account and shows their first password once, for you to hand over.

Adapt's final approval needs two people besides the one who asks, each a workspace owner or auditor, and at least one of them an auditor. Choose the audit role for that person when you add them.

What you have at the end

  • One project record per agent. Every answer, version, approval request, decision, build, review and change is an entry, signed and linked to the one before it. Each decision names exactly which version was approved.
  • The approved documents. The brief, the build plan, the plan of controls and, when the release step can run, the signed release package and the operator's signed notes.
  • A record you can check. amc a4 verify checks every entry against keys you choose to trust. amc a4 export writes the record to one signed file that someone without your keys can check.

What A4 Forge will not tell you

  • That the agent is safe, correct or lawful. A4 keeps the record of how the agent was built. People judge the agent.
  • That a signed record is true. A signature shows who wrote an entry and that nobody has changed it since. Every A4 record is self-reported.
  • How the agent behaves. A build on the stand-in model shows the tools ran. It says nothing about how the agent behaves with a real model.
  • That a rule applies to you. Pack content and Adapt's legal notes are AI-drafted, and no lawyer has reviewed them. Check each rule with your own advisers.
  • That an outsider checked it. Nobody outside your workspace reviews an A4 record yet, and a second approver in your workspace is not an independent review.
  • That it was deployed anywhere else. Today the release step only restarts AMC on the same machine that runs it under Docker Compose or Helm. Other places to deploy to are planned.

How to try it

Build AMC from source (see Get AMC). The published 1.1.1 release does not have A4 Forge. It is on as soon as AMC starts.

In the Compass Console

  1. Type amc in a terminal and choose Build an agent for my industry, or choose Open the console in my browser and sign in.
  2. On Home, the first card is Build an agent for your industry. Pick the industry and the pack closest to the work, choose who approves each step, name the project and press Start building.
  3. Each project page opens with What to do now: the four steps, where you are, one sentence on what is needed, and one button that does it.
  4. Every empty answer shows an example. Use an example fills the empty answers with sample text marked (sample), for you to change before you save.
  5. When a step needs approval, the page says how many people must approve, which role they need, who may not approve, and who has approved so far.

In a terminal

Start AMC and add the people who will approve. Each person then signs in with their own name.

amc up
amc user init --username <you>
amc user add --username <second> --role OWNER,APPROVER
amc user add --username <third> --role APPROVER,AUDITOR

The terminal can list projects, say what to do next, approve and check a record. Starting a project and running its steps happen in the Compass Console.

amc a4 list
amc a4 guide <projectId>    # what to do next, with the exact approve command
amc a4 verify <projectId> --allow-unpinned --allow-unanchored

Without keys you chose to trust, amc a4 verify only checks that nothing changed and never says "trusted". To turn A4 Forge off, stop AMC with amc down and start it again with AMC_A4_PREVIEW=0 amc up: then the console says agent projects are turned off, and every amc a4 command stops with an error. The A4 Forge guide has every command and option.

Questions

Is my data sent anywhere?

The record stays in your workspace on your own machine. Free text that may hold personal data, such as comments and free answers in the brief, is kept encrypted in the project's own store, and an owner can erase it. Erasing takes full effect once every older backup has been replaced.

The build session runs on the AI model you pick. The stand-in model stays on your machine; a hosted model's provider receives that session's prompts. At the release step AMC checks once that the deployed address answers. An export is not encrypted, so keep it under your control.

Can I use my own AI model?

Yes, for Assemble's build session: a model from OpenAI, Anthropic, DeepSeek or Gemini, or a local Ollama model, once that provider is set up. A4 records whether a provider is set up, never its key. Adapt's test of the agent's context always runs on the stand-in model.

What does a signature show?

Who wrote an entry, and that it has not changed since. Never that what it says is true. To trust who signed, check the record against keys you chose in advance.

Why is it a preview?

Some parts are not finished: nobody outside your workspace reviews a record yet, the release step only restarts AMC on the same machine, and the legal notes have not been reviewed by an expert. It is on by default so you can try it; starting AMC with AMC_A4_PREVIEW=0 turns it off.
