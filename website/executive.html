AMC for Executives · board brief

Know how each AI agent was built, and whether it is ready for real work

AMC helps your teams build an AI agent for regulated work in four steps, with a named person's sign-off on each. Then it watches the agent while it works, keeps a signed record of what it did, and scores it from that record. For each agent you get a level, the record behind it, and a list of fixes.

What The Board Sees

  • For each new agent, who asked for it, who approved each step of its build, and exactly which version they approved.
  • Each agent's level and how its score changes over time.
  • The biggest risks, and which actions the agent is not yet allowed to take alone.
  • How much of the agent's work is recorded, whether the records are intact, and whether they are ready for an audit.
  • The EU AI Act risk class, where it applies.
  • L3 business-risk memo for deciding on limited use.

What Engineering Does

  • Builds new agents with A4 Forge, AMC's guided build, so every step has a named approver.
  • Runs amc in the folder where an agent works and connects its activity logs and rules.
  • Follows AMC's advice to fix weak spots, then scores again.
  • Exports reports, audit files, and build checks that block a release until it passes.

What Changes

  • What a vendor says is kept apart from what the agent was seen doing.
  • Accepted risks become tracked tasks, not meeting notes.
  • An agent gets more freedom only when its record supports it.
  • Compliance teams get records they can check again, not screenshots.

When your team builds a new agent

A4 Forge, a preview that is on by default, takes one agent through four steps. No step moves on until the people your team names approve it, and every step is written to a signed record.

  1. Say what it is for. The brief: the problem, who it serves, what it must never do, how risky the work is.
  2. Build it. Two people, neither of whom built it, approve the result.
  3. Fit it to your rules. Two people other than the one who asks approve it, each a workspace owner or auditor.
  4. Release it. AMC prepares a signed release package and your operator deploys it.

A second approver in your own workspace keeps duties apart; it is not an independent review. No A4 record says an agent is safe, correct or lawful. How A4 Forge works

From first score to a record you can show the board_

  1. Inventory. List your agents, their owners, their tools, the data they can reach, and what they may do alone.
  2. Score. Run the default questions. Answers backed by a record count for more than claims.
  3. Fix. Rank the gaps by risk, compliance impact, and effort.
  4. Govern. Use reports, audit files, and release checks to keep each agent within what you approved.
