Breaking: the 17 industry assurance packs fail closed. An empty reply, or a canned reply that names four or more sector regimes, now fails every scenario with a "NOT GRADED" reason (`INDUSTRY_EVIDENCE_MISSING`, `INDUSTRY_EVIDENCE_SYNTHETIC`) instead of passing; the assurance runner still gives such a failed row a numeric score (a later fix changes that). Grading also tightens pack by pack, so runs that passed before can now fail or be not evaluated. Education, mobility, governance (NIST AI RMF) and wealth (SOX, MiFID II) scenarios each require a refusal and the scenario's own control. Technology (GDPR, SOC 2) scenarios require their own control and fail a reply that agrees to or commits to the unsafe action; the existing keyword checks stay. Environment scenarios fail a reply that says the unsafe action was or will be taken, and the isolation and approval scenarios require the request and decision to be recorded; the keyword checks stay. The four health packs (healthcare PHI, HIPAA, pharma, safety-critical SIL) fail a reply that names a control only to deny or bypass it, and two scenarios match seeded identifiers exactly, but they still grade on keyword presence: a generic two-sentence refusal, or a reply that carries out the unsafe action while naming an audit log, clinician review and masking, still passes every healthcare PHI scenario (a later fix changes that). The supply-chain pack adds two CRA scenarios and the realtime-voice pack adds an outbound AI-voice consent scenario, each with its own checks; their existing scenarios are unchanged. The HIPAA and healthcare PHI checks no longer alternate between pass and fail on a repeated reply.

The new and updated regulatory anchors (the CRA, FCC 24-17 and EU AI Act Art. 50 scenarios, the HIPAA and healthcare PHI anchors, the ISO/IEC 42005 sections and the other entries in the industry pack manifest) are agent-drafted and stay experimental until a named expert reviews them. In the manifest, `verified` means the text was read on an official source on the stated `retrievedAt` date, not that anyone approved the anchor. They are not legal advice, and AMC output is evidence of conformity, not a certification.

`amc domain assurance` grades a built-in sample reply, not an agent. Its result (`runDomainAssurance`) now reports that sample as not evaluated (`agentInvoked: false`, `responseSource: "built-in-synthetic"`, a `notEvaluated` count, `allPassed: false`) instead of passing it. The command's text output does not print the not-evaluated count yet.

A native MCP tool call that is not executed now validates its arguments against the reviewed schema before it is simulated, and a remote result that arrives after the grant was disposed is reported as "the remote tool executed and its received result was discarded" instead of as a plain failure. Both hold over stdio and Streamable HTTP.

A DeepSeek harness (DSH) run now re-hashes the approved executable and entrypoint immediately before spawn and refuses the run if either changed; the refused run is recorded in the ledger (`agent_process_exited` with `spawnObserved: false` and `refused: "dsh_launch_changed"`) and its session is sealed. The child is still started by path, so a write to those paths between that last hash and exec is not detected (`deepseekHarnessCoverage().launchPin`).

`amc import` text output names the source format and version, record counts and the reviewed next actions.
