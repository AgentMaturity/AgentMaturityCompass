Regulatory incident clocks now run against stored incidents. `amc incident clocks <id> --station <station> [--now <iso>] [--json]` lists the station's reporting clocks (due date, status, source, review status). With `--trigger <TRIGGER> --at <iso>` or `--notified <clockId> --at <iso>` it first records a signed clock event: the time given is the operator's claim, and AMC stores when and through which surface (`cli` or `api`) it was recorded. AMC refuses an event dated before the incident or more than 5 minutes after it was recorded, an unknown trigger or clock, a second notice for a clock, and a notice before its trigger; a stored row whose signature or guards fail is refused by its event id. `amc incident oversight <id> --decision <decision> --reviewer <id> --rationale <text> [--clock <clockId>...]` appends a human-oversight record signed with the workspace auditor key; the reviewer id is stored as stated. `amc incident show <id> --packet <dir> --station <station>` writes the regulator evidence packet as `<id>.packet.json` and `<id>.packet.md`. The API adds `GET /api/v1/incidents/:id/clocks`, `POST /api/v1/incidents/:id/clock-events` and `POST /api/v1/incidents/:id/oversight` (400 on bad input, 404 on an unknown incident), which Studio reaches through its API delegation, and the MCP server adds the read-only `amc_incident_clocks` tool (11 tools). Signatures verify against the workspace's own key history: a local audit trail, not proof that a claimed time is true or of who reviewed.

The clock table gains five clocks read from primary text on 2026-10-07: 23 NYCRR 500.17 (72-hour notice to the NYDFS superintendent after determining a cybersecurity incident; 24-hour notice and 30-day written explanation after an extortion payment), 16 CFR 314.4(j) (FTC notice within 30 days of discovering a notification event affecting 500 or more consumers) and 31 CFR 1020.320 (SAR within 30 calendar days of initial detection, never disclosed). The two Texas § 521.053 clocks are now checked against the statute and marked verified. Every clock carries the review status "experimental: agent-drafted, expert review pending (D-08)"; AMC computes and records deadlines, does not check conditions such as the number of consumers affected, does not file notices, and gives no legal advice.

Security: oversight records carry a server-set `recordedTs` and are appended only after the whole file passes `verifyOversightChain` (every record, the `prevRecordHash` links and non-decreasing review times), so a forged, edited, deleted or reordered record is never endorsed and the evidence packet reports a broken chain as missing evidence; clock events take `recordedTs` from the server clock instead of the caller, and the first-recorded notice for a clock wins, so a later row cannot turn a missed deadline into a met one.
